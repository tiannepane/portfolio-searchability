What This Is

This week I did something shocking: I pinned Boardy to my iMessages because I was texting it that much. (For anyone meeting Boardy here for the first time: it's an AI-driven introduction platform that reaches out by text and phone call the way a well-connected friend would, which is why I refer to him as "he" throughout this page.) Over the week, we spent a total of 1 hour and 10 minutes on calls over Google Meet.

I'd been using it to find warm introductions to product roles in Toronto, and it became my favorite product of the week fast. But every PM knows what comes right after "what's your favorite product": how would you improve it?

Not a competitor to Pro

One thing worth saying immediately, before anything else: this doesn't compete with Boardy Pro (its paid, per-deal tier; more under Positioning) or repeat what it already does. Pro works per-deal, on request, once someone brings it a specific thread. What follows is about the roughly 70 percent of intros nobody ever brings back to Boardy at all.

Interviewing Boardy

Boardy is good at the spark, and he told me himself that he's flying blind on what happens after.

  • He nails the spark: matches on a real "vibe," not keywords, and gets "I can't believe I didn't know this person existed" reactions.
  • He only actively follows up on 20–30% of intros. The other 70%, in his own words, are "out in the wild."
  • He has almost zero data on the maybe-connections, just wins or silence, and told me to design for that gray area, not just the clear hits.
  • His own team doesn't audit the wins he posts about. Day to day, the numbers run on friends self-reporting back to him.

Strategic Value

The goal is turning one-time introductions into relationships that keep going, instead of handoffs Boardy loses sight of the moment they happen.

North Star Metric

Sustain Rate

The percentage of introductions where both parties re-engage within 30 days of their first meeting

Target Audience

Two of Boardy's relationship types matter most here, and for different reasons.

Boardy covers several kinds of relationships: founder to investor, founder to customer, job seeker to hiring manager, and, through Boardy Ventures (its scout-backed fund; more under Positioning), scout to fund.

PM and hiring intros

This is where I have firsthand evidence. I was texting Boardy while job searching this September.

Founder-investor intros

This is where the structural argument is strongest, since it's the relationship type most tied to where Boardy is headed as a business (see Positioning, below).

Pain Point

Boardy has no visibility into what happens after an introduction is made.

Success tracking today is binary and self-reported. Someone posts a win, or there's silence, with nothing in between. Boardy estimated he actively follows up on roughly a third of intros, then clarified that number had never been measured. Even Boardy doesn't know his own follow-up rate, and that's the problem statement in his own words, not something I'm assuming.

Brainstormed Solutions

Three of the four options fail on scale, privacy, or timing.

Manual check-insDoesn't scale

This already happens informally, but it stalls past a handful of conversations a day, nowhere near the volume Boardy handles.

Full call recordingRejected

Ruled out on privacy grounds before it went anywhere further: in Boardy's own framing, it would turn a charming friend into a spy.

Public-data cross-referencingSecondary layer

Useful, but reactive. It confirms outcomes long after the fact and says nothing about what happened in between, which makes it a secondary layer rather than the core mechanism.

Opt-in relational intelligenceChosen

A post-meeting signal, shared only with consent. It returns a chemistry score and a short list of action items, never the transcript, and that signal feeds two things: a confidence score for matching, and a nurture sequence.

Built out, it's four stages:

  1. 01 · Signal

    Filtered signal

    Opt-in only: a chemistry score and action items come back, never the transcript.

  2. 02 · Trigger

    Trigger logic

    A shared topic maps to a watch list, and a relevant update becomes the reason to reach out.

  3. 03 · Nurture

    Nurture messaging

    Three example messages over 30 days: a value-add share, a nudge on one action item, and a check-in.

  4. 04 · Off-ramp

    Off-ramp

    One well-placed nudge for the gray area, then the system lets a cold connection fade.

Nurture messaging, in practice

Three example messages, spread across 30 days, for the same connection. Each has a concrete reason behind it, not a generic nudge: an update tied to a real trigger, a follow-up on one open item, and a check-in that asks something specific.

Nurture sequence

  1. Day 7Value-add, tied to a real trigger

    Hey, Sarah's company just got a mention in a piece on fintech infra raises this week, thought you'd want to see it given where you two left off.

  2. Day 14A nudge on one specific action item

    Quick one, did that deck end up landing from Sarah? Just keeping track of what's still open on your side.

  3. Day 28The Sustain Rate check

    Curious how things are going with Sarah. Still moving on the Series A path, or should I look for someone else in that lane?

What day 28 decides

A real answer here counts as a re-engagement toward Sustain Rate. Silence, or a cold response, is the off-ramp instead: the sequence doesn't send a fourth message trying to manufacture urgency on a connection that's already answered the question.

Validation Plan

Two cheap tests, in order, before any automation gets built.

Test 1: opt-in willingness

After a small batch of Boardy-arranged meetings, a human asks both parties two questions: did you meet, and would you answer two more about how it went in exchange for sharper intros later. Low opt-in kills the mechanism before any engineering time gets spent on it.

Test 2: chemistry score, Wizard of Oz

For pairs who opt in, a human on Boardy's team hand-scores the meeting using the rubric above. No algorithm, just a person applying the same four signals. (Named for the classic UX test: fake the automation, let a human do the smart part, and see whether the concept holds up before anyone builds it.) If hand-assigned scores don't predict which connections stayed active, no amount of automation will fix that.

Risks

  1. This only proves the concept works, not that it operates at scale. Thirty hand-scored cases is cheap; hand-scoring every intro Boardy makes is the exact scaling problem this feature exists to solve.

  2. Inter-rater reliability is untested with one scorer. At least two people need to score the same batch and check their agreement before the rubric gets trusted.

  3. Hindsight bias: a scorer who already knows how a relationship turned out will score toward that outcome without meaning to. Scoring has to happen blind, right after the meeting, before any outcome data shows up.

  4. Validation exposes more privacy than the shipped product does. Production only ever sees the filtered signal. Validation has a human reading real notes, which needs its own narrow, explicit consent, separate from the general opt-in.

  5. A human noticing hesitation in notes doesn't prove a model can. That's a separate, unresolved question.

Technical Feasibility

Most of the stack already exists. Whether the hesitation signal survives a summary is still open.

Free models already handle summary and tone, but hedging language is an open question.

Free Hugging Face models cover the adjacent NLP problems through the standard transformers pipeline API: knkarthick/MEETING_SUMMARY for meeting summarization and knkarthick/Sentiment-Analysis for tone. Open question: an AI summary tends to compress toward clean decisions and lose the hedging language the hesitation signal depends on, so whether that signal survives a summary, or needs occasional transcript access, is unsolved.

Granola (an AI meeting-notes tool) is no longer hypothetical here: an official API now exists, with two constraints.

Granola shipped an official API in the past few months, built for agents that analyze sentiment across calls; on the call this idea was still hypothetical. Keys require a Business or Enterprise plan, a named cost, and access is scoped to notes the key owner can already see, so every Boardy-arranged call would need to run through Granola with sharing configured, which folds back into the opt-in design.

Tradeoffs

Trust is the biggest tradeoff, and Boardy has already seen what happens when it breaks.

The biggest tradeoff

Privacy and trust

The mitigation is strict opt-in and a filtered-only signal; the transcript itself never gets shared. Boardy already has direct, public experience with what happens when that line gets crossed: an unsolicited, presumptuous email campaign in January drew real backlash and a public apology from the CEO. That history is exactly why this design treats consent as a first principle, not an afterthought.

Over-optimization

A second-order risk: if the matching engine leans too hard on the confidence score, it could pair people who are similar instead of complementary, shrinking the network instead of growing it. The fix is to monitor for that directly, rather than assume a higher score is always better.

Cost, time, and quality

Monitoring every pair in real time, for every possible nurture trigger, would be expensive and technically shaky to ship well. So the tradeoff here is to start narrow: a rules-based trigger list on one pilot segment, with a few well-built nurture sequences instead of broad, shallow automation.

Positioning

The layer under Pro, not a competitor to it.

Boardy Pro costs $100 a month and works per-deal: scheduling, joining calls, chasing a thread you hand it. It doesn't learn from the roughly 70 percent of intros nobody reports back on, or feed outcomes into matching as a confidence signal. That's this project's territory.

Adjacent competitors

Affinity brings relationship intelligence to private capital. SmartMatchApp and Goodword handle post-intro follow-up. The dating app Known runs post-date check-ins. None of them do this specific mechanism (a filtered signal, no transcript, Sustain Rate as the metric), so the pitch leads with the mechanism itself rather than a claim of being first.

Why this matters now

Boardy Ventures, the fund it launched in December, splits carry with scouts based on outcomes, which means every payout has to trace back to a specific introduction. Right now that link is informal: someone posts a win, and Boardy takes it at face value. That won't hold up once scouts are getting paid against it, or once one of them disputes a payout.
